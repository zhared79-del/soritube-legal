How Coffee Travels the World

Your morning cup of coffee starts its journey on a farm far away. Coffee grows on small trees in warm regions near the equator, often called the coffee belt.

The fruit of the coffee tree is a small red cherry. Inside each cherry are usually two seeds. These seeds are what we call coffee beans.

After harvest, the beans are dried and cleaned. At this stage they are green and have almost no coffee smell.

The green beans are shipped around the world. Roasters then heat them until they turn brown and develop the rich aroma we love.

Finally, a barista grinds the beans and brews them into the cup in your hands. So every cup connects farmers, travelers, roasters, and you.
